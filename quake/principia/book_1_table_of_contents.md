# Book 1 — De motu corporum (On the motion of bodies)

> Table of contents reconstructed from Wikipedia, since the 1846 Motte translation does not include one.
> This covers motion in the absence of any resisting medium.

---

## Section 1 — Mathematical Lemmas: "The Method of First and Last Ratios"

The book opens with a collection of mathematical lemmas on the method of first and last ratios — a geometrical form of infinitesimal calculus.

**Folder:** `geometrical_form_of_infinitesimal_calculus/`

---

## Section 2 — Centripetal Forces and Kepler's Second Law

Establishes relationships between centripetal forces and the law of areas now known as Kepler's second law, and relates circular velocity and radius of path-curvature to radial force, and relationships between centripetal forces varying as the inverse-square of the distance to the center and orbits of conic-section form.

- **Propositions 1–3:** Centripetal forces and the law of areas (Kepler's second law)
- **Proposition 4:** Circular velocity and radius of path-curvature related to radial force
- **Propositions 5–10:** Inverse-square centripetal forces and conic-section orbits

**Folder:** `centripetal_forces_and_keplers_second_law/`

---

## Section 3 — Eccentric Conic Sections (Propositions 11–31)

Properties of motion in paths of eccentric conic-section form including ellipses, and their relationship with inverse-square central forces directed to a focus. Includes Newton's theorem about ovals (Lemma 28).

**Folder:** `eccentric_conic_sections/`

---

## Section 4 — Motion of Apses (Propositions 43–45)

Demonstration that in an eccentric orbit under centripetal force where the apse may move, a steady non-moving orientation of the line of apses is an indicator of an inverse-square law of force.

**Folder:** `motion_of_apses/`

---

## Section 5 — Motion of Bodies Drawn to One Another (Propositions 57–69)

Deals with the motion of bodies drawn to one another by centripetal forces. Of primary interest for its application to the Solar System. Includes Proposition 66 with its 22 corollaries — Newton's first steps in defining the three-body problem.

**Folder:** `bodies_drawn_to_one_another/`

---

## Section 6 — Attractive Forces of Spherical Bodies (Propositions 70–84)

Deals with the attractive forces of spherical bodies. Contains Newton's proof that a massive spherically symmetrical body attracts other bodies outside itself as if all its mass were concentrated at its centre — the Shell theorem. Enables the inverse square law of gravitation to be applied to the real solar system.

**Folder:** `attractive_forces_of_spherical_bodies/`
