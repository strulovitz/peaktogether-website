Below sits the \textcolor{figbteal}{second figure $PprT$ with its own rank of three parallelograms} on the equal bases $PQ$, $QR$, $RT$ of \textcolor{basegreen}{the baseline $PT$}. Notice that \textcolor{curvered}{the curve $prT$} leans the other way --- it descends from $p$ toward $T$ --- so this figure is shaped quite differently from the first. Yet it carries the \emph{same count} of strips, which we name $q_1$, $q_2$, $q_3$ in order from $P$ toward $T$. The whole point of Lemma IV is that the figures may differ wildly in form, provided their corresponding strips agree in ultimate ratio.
