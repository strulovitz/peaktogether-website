\noindent \textbf{vi} \hfill \textit{Introduction to Linear Algebra}

\bigskip

That picture illustrated two basic operations---adding vectors $a_1 + a_2$ and multiplying
a vector by $2$. Combining those operations produced a ``linear combination'' $2a_1 + a_2$:

\bigskip

\begin{center}
\fbox{\parbox{0.7\textwidth}{\centering
\textbf{Linear combination} $= ca_1 + da_2$ \ for any numbers $c$ and $d$
}}
\end{center}

\bigskip

Those numbers $c$ and $d$ can be negative. In that case $ca_1$ and $da_2$ will reverse their direc-
tions: they go right to left. Also very important, $c$ and $d$ can involve fractions. Here is a
picture with a lot more linear combinations. \textbf{Eventually we want all vectors} $ca_1 + da_2$.

\bigskip
\bigskip

\bigskip
\bigskip

Here is the key! \textbf{The combinations $ca_1 + da_2$ fill a whole plane.} It is an infinite plane
in 3-dimensional space. By using more and more fractions and decimals $c$ and $d$, we fill in
a complete plane. \emph{Every point on the plane is a combination of $a_1$ and $a_2$}.

\bigskip

Now comes a fundamental idea in linear algebra: \textbf{a matrix}. The matrix $A$ holds
$n$ column vectors $a_1, a_2, \dots, a_n$. At this point our matrix has two columns $a_1$ and $a_2$, and
those are vectors in 3-dimensional space. So the matrix has \textbf{three rows and two columns}.

\bigskip

\begin{center}
\fbox{\parbox{0.75\textwidth}{
\begin{tabular}{@{}l@{\hspace{1.5cm}}c@{}}
\begin{minipage}{4cm}
3 by 2 matrix \\
$m = 3$ rows \\
$n = 2$ columns
\end{minipage}
&
$A = \begin{bmatrix} a_1 & a_2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 3 & 4 \\ 1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}$
\end{tabular}
}}
\end{center}

\bigskip

The combinations of those two columns produced a plane in three-dimensional space.
There is a natural name for that plane. \textbf{It is the column space of the matrix.} For any $A$,
\textbf{the column space of $A$ contains all combinations of the columns.}

\bigskip

Here are the four ideas introduced so far. You will see them all in Chapter 1.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Column vectors $a_1$ and $a_2$ in three dimensions
    \item Linear combinations $ca_1 + da_2$ of those vectors
    \item The matrix $A$ contains the columns $a_1$ and $a_2$
    \item Column space of the matrix $=$ \emph{all linear combinations of the columns} $=$ \emph{plane}
\end{enumerate}
