Path 2: Unconventional Correlated Superconductors (Cuprates and Beyond)

Idea: Leverage strong electron–electron interactions and magnetism in transition-metal compounds to find new high-Tc states.

Rationale: The 1986 discovery of 35 K superconductivity in La₂CuO₄-based oxides (cuprates) shattered assumptions and opened a new era. By 1993, Hg-based cuprates reached 133 K at ambient pressure (and up to ~164 K under modest pressure) – records for ambient-pressure superconductors. These materials involve layered copper-oxide planes, doping-induced Mott-insulator to metal transitions, and an unconventional pairing mechanism likely driven by spin-fluctuations (magnetic interactions) rather than phonons. The lack of a complete theory notwithstanding, their very existence (with d-wave Cooper pairs) proves that electron correlations can produce high Tc. Researchers have since discovered iron-based superconductors (up to ~55 K) and, more recently, nickelate oxides (Nd₀.₈Sr₀.₂NiO₂ with Tc ~15 K) that are electronically analogous to cuprates. While cuprates themselves seem to have plateaued (no clear progress beyond ~150 K despite immense effort), the hope is that exploring related materials (nickelates, layered pnictides, perhaps new mixed-anion compounds) or tuning spin-mediated pairing could inch closer to room T.

Prerequisites: Strong-correlation physics (Hubbard model, Mott insulators), knowledge of phase diagrams (antiferromagnetism, pseudogap, charge order in cuprates), and familiarity with advanced experimental probes (e.g. ARPES, neutron scattering) to identify pairing signatures.

Dependencies: This path might benefit from Path 3 (heterostructures) – e.g. applying pressure or strain (Path 1/3) to squeeze more performance from cuprate-like systems – and from theoretical insights (Path 9) into how magnetic pairing scales with coupling strength.

Signs of Progress: Key milestones would be discovering a new superconducting family with Tc beyond cuprates’ 135 K record at ambient. For example, a higher-Tc nickelate or a hypothetical “cuprate 2.0” with different cations. Any verified superconductor above 150 K at ambient pressure via this route would signal progress. On the microscopic side, evidence of stronger pairing correlations (e.g. higher superfluid density, shorter coherence lengths approaching room T scales) in correlated materials would indicate the path is on track. Ultimately, a material in this class reaching even ~200 K would strongly support the feasibility of this approach.

Base Camp 2.1: Mott Insulators & Hubbard Model

  • Nevill F. Mott. Metal-Insulator Transitions. Taylor & Francis, 2nd ed., 1990. – Classic monograph by Mott, explaining electron correlation and localization. Provides insight into why undoped cuprates are insulating and sets stage for understanding how adding carriers leads to superconductivity.
  • Patrick Fazekas. Lecture Notes on Electron Correlation and Magnetism. World Scientific, 1999. – Covers the Hubbard and t-J models, magnetism, and basic many-body theory in a pedagogical way. Great for learning how strong correlations give rise to magnetically mediated pairing and other phenomena.
  • A. J. Leggett. “What Do We Know About High-Tc?” Nature Physics, vol. 2, 2006, pp. 134–136. – Brief perspective by Nobel laureate Tony Leggett, summarizing the state of understanding of cuprates two decades in. Useful as a reality check on how correlation physics defies simple explanations, and highlighting key experimental facts any theory must explain.

Base Camp 2.2: Cuprate Phenomenology

  • J. Orenstein and A. J. Millis. “Advances in the Physics of High-Tc Superconductivity.” Science, vol. 288, 2000, pp. 468–474. – Excellent review of cuprate experiments (charge/spin order, pseudogap, symmetry of order parameter). Written in a clear style that links theoretical concepts to data. Helps newcomers grasp what’s unusual about cuprates versus BCS superconductors.
  • C. C. Tsuei and J. R. Kirtley. “Pairing Symmetry in Cuprate Superconductors.” Reviews of Modern Physics, vol. 72, no. 4, 2000, pp. 969–1016. – Comprehensive review of phase-sensitive experiments that established d-wave pairing in cuprates. This is instructive to learn how one proves a particular pairing symmetry – a critical piece in understanding unconventional SC.
  • B. Keimer, S. A. Kivelson, M. R. Norman, et al. “From Quantum Matter to High-Temperature Superconductivity in Copper Oxides.” Nature, vol. 518, 2015, pp. 179–186. – Contemporary review linking various intertwined orders (spin stripes, charge order, nematicity) with superconductivity in cuprates. It highlights what new experiments (resonant X-ray, neutron scattering) have revealed. Useful for forming a modern view of cuprate complexity.

Base Camp 2.3: Unconventional Pairing Mechanisms

  • P. A. Lee, N. Nagaosa, and X.-G. Wen. “Doping a Mott Insulator: Physics of High-Tc Superconductivity.” Reviews of Modern Physics, vol. 78, 2006, pp. 17–85. – Influential theoretical review that presents the case for spin-mediated pairing and the “plain vanilla” Hubbard model approach to cuprates. Provides both intuitive pictures and technical results (e.g. RVB theory). Good for a deep theoretical perspective on how Mott physics yields d-wave SC.
  • D. J. Scalapino. “A Common Thread: The Pairing Interaction for Unconventional Superconductors.” Reviews of Modern Physics, vol. 84, 2012, pp. 1383–1417. – Overview arguing that many unconventional superconductors (cuprates, pnictides, heavy fermions) share a similar pairing glue: antiferromagnetic spin fluctuations. Presents a unifying picture with calculations of the spin susceptibility and its effect on pairing. Helps us compare different materials under one framework.
  • K. Ishida, Y. Nakai, and H. Hosono. “To What Extent Iron-Pnictide New Superconductors Have Been Clarified: A Progress Report.” Journal of the Physical Society of Japan, vol. 78, 2009, 062001. – Early but detailed account of iron-based superconductors, including discussion of s± pairing symmetry due to spin fluctuations. Useful to contrast with cuprates – shows another flavor of magnetically mediated SC, and the importance of multi-band effects (relevant for complex correlated systems).

Base Camp 2.4: Iron-Based and Other Families

  • John Paglione and Richard L. Greene. “High-Temperature Superconductivity in Iron-Based Materials.” Nature Physics, vol. 6, 2010, pp. 645–658. – Well-written review from shortly after the discovery of Fe-pnictides. Covers materials, phase diagrams, and compares to cuprates. Great for drawing parallels (structure: layered, antiferromagnetic parent, etc.) and differences (multi-orbital, s± gap) with cuprates.
  • D. C. Johnston. “The Puzzle of High-Tc Superconductivity in Fe Pnictides and Chalcogenides.” Advances in Physics, vol. 59, 2010, pp. 803–1061. – Lengthy, detailed review (100+ pages) compiling experimental properties of Fe-based superconductors. It’s a trove of data (specific heat, penetration depth, etc.), helping one practice applying concepts learned in BC2.1–2.3 to a new family. It underscores how multifaceted high-Tc problems are.
  • Qimiao Si, Rong Yu, and Elihu Abrahams. “High Temperature Superconductivity in Iron Pnictides and Chalcogenides.” Nature Reviews Materials, vol. 1, 2016, 16017. – Perspective focusing on the theoretical understanding of Fe-based SC, especially the role of electronic correlations and possible quantum criticality. Useful to see how a well-studied new high-Tc family reached a theoretical consensus (or lack thereof) and to inform how we might approach any new family that could be discovered.

Base Camp 2.5: Emergent New Correlated Superconductors

  • Danfeng Li, et al. “Superconductivity in an Infinite-Layer Nickelate.” Nature, vol. 572, 2019, pp. 624–627. – The discovery paper for Nd0.8Sr0.2NiO2 (nickelate) superconductivity. Key for understanding the materials science of how a new analog of cuprates was made (film synthesis, oxygen reduction). It invites thinking: could nickelates be tuned to higher Tc?
  • George A. Sawatzky. “Superconductivity Seen in a Non-magnetic Nickel Oxide.” Nature, vol. 572, 2019, pp. 592–593. – A News & Views commentary on the above discovery, giving context on how nickelates compare to cuprates. It’s instructive to see what experts highlight (e.g. the role of missing Cu 3d9 spin-½ in Ni’s 3d9 configuration). Helps formulate what questions to ask when a new correlated SC appears.
  • Andrew S. Botana and Julia Y. Choi. “Experimental Progress in Superconducting Nickelates.” Annual Review of Condensed Matter Physics, vol. 14, 2023, pp. 99–122. – Up-to-date review of the nickelate superconductors, covering developments up to very recent times. Useful for seeing how quickly a new material class evolves (multiple doping recipes, new compounds like Nd6Ni5O12, etc.) and what challenges remain (e.g. making bulk samples). This can guide how we approach future “new superconductors” that might emerge from our efforts.
