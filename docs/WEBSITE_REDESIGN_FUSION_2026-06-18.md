## The core diagnosis (why she didn't "get it")

Your old homepage led with poetry and metaphor — "turn the biggest unsolved problems into beautiful interactive adventures." That's beautiful, but it's abstract. The human brain understands new things by anchoring them to familiar concrete nouns. The most familiar, concrete things you have are:

    Free video games (everyone knows what that is)
    Remakes of classic games people loved (instant nostalgia + recognition)
    You play it with someone, snuggled at one computer

So the fix is simple: lead with those, demote the mountain metaphor to second place, and let the romance be a quiet wink in the brand name — never a billboard. The mountain is the flavor; the games are the product.

Here's the one sentence everything should be built backward from — your North Star:

    Free download-and-play games — remakes of classic '90s games — where you and your partner climb the hardest unsolved problems in science together at one computer.

If a visitor understands that in 5 seconds, you've won.

## 6. Honest critique — the things that will still cost you users (and how to fix them)

These are the issues that matter most. I'm being direct because you asked.

**⚠️ 1 — python app.py is your single biggest threat.** This is the one that will silently kill most of your traffic. The vast majority of 15–25-year-olds have never opened a terminal, don't have Python installed, and will hit a wall the moment something needs pip install pygame. Your whole "cozy and accessible" brand collapses at that command.

    Fix, in order of impact: (a) Browser-playable demo — Pygame can run in-browser via pygbag/WebAssembly. "Try it instantly, no install" next to the download button would transform your conversion. (b) One-click executables — use PyInstaller to ship a .exe/.app so non-coders just double-click; keep the python app.py path for the GitHub crowd. (c) At minimum, a screenshot-by-screenshot "Install in 2 minutes" guide — and include a requirements.txt + pip install -r requirements.txt step, because missing dependencies are the #1 silent failure your current pitch ignores.

**⚠️ 2 — The romantic innuendo is a dial, and right now it's turned up too high.** "Peak/climax together" is cute to some, but it risks (a) being read as creepy or not-serious by the science-curious audience you want, and (b) being inappropriate for classrooms, libraries, and parent-shared computers — which kills your best free distribution channel (teachers). And remember: your audience includes 15–17-year-olds, where romance + minors is genuinely sensitive.

    Fix: Keep it as a wink, never a billboard. Let the brand name PeakTogether + a couple-under-a-blanket image carry the joke quietly. Never write the word "climax" on the site. Make the wholesome surface meaning 100% self-sufficient, so someone who never gets the joke still has a perfectly coherent experience. (Notice I removed "the brain is the largest sexual organ" line from the hero — that single line is what tips it from "cute" to "uncomfortable in a classroom.")

**⚠️ 3 — "Couples only" excludes most of your audience.** Ages 15–25 includes tons of single people, friend pairs, siblings, and classmates. "For couples" cuts your market in half and makes everyone else feel it's "not for them."

    Fix: Reframe from couples to "built for two." The romance becomes the flavor, not the gate. Add one line everywhere: "Designed for two — couples, friends, siblings, classmates. Playing solo? You can drive both roles yourself." That one sentence recovers your entire solo audience.

**⚠️ 4 — Calling them "remakes of Descent / Mortal Kombat / C&C" is a trademark risk.** Those are live trademarks. Using them in game titles (like "DESCENT QED") or copying their assets could invite legal trouble.

    Fix: Use "inspired by" / "a tribute to the classic [genre]" in copy. Keep original names out of your game titles where you can — consider renaming the flagship to something like "QED Descent: Proof Flight" (keeps the pun, drops the direct brand collision). And the Prompt D template above already tells the image model to treat old screenshots as loose reference, not a copy — keep that wording.

**⚠️ 5 — Clarify what it means to "win" an unsolved problem.** A sharp visitor will ask: "If Riemann is unsolved, how do I beat the game?"

    Fix: Say it explicitly: "You won't solve the impossible tonight. Our games take you up to Base Camp — the established proofs and ideas you need to truly understand the peak. You learn the trail, so you can finally stare at the summit with real understanding." Also use "real concepts from actual proofs" rather than "real proofs" — it's more honest for a 10-minute session.

**⚠️ 6 — Soften the violence language for the public-facing site.** "Weapons," "destroy robots," "fire missiles" reads less wholesome to the parents and teachers you want on your side.

    Fix: On the website, use "tools," "idea-beams," "resolve the robot," "clear the path." The game can still feel action-packed; the marketing copy just needs to sound friendly.

**⚠️ 7 — Two small ones worth fixing now:**

    Controller is a hidden hardware gate. Many people don't own a controller. Say clearly that keyboard + mouse, two keyboards, or keyboard + controller all work.
    Color encodes the proof's dependency structure — beautiful idea, but it locks out colorblind players. Add a redundant cue (shapes, labels, or patterns) alongside color so the structure survives without color vision.
