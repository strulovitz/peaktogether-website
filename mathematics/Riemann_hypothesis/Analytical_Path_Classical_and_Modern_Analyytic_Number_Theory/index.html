📐 Path 1: Classical & Modern Analytic Number Theory

Using complex analysis and analytic number theory to study the zeta function directly.

This path builds from fundamental tools up to advanced results about zeta's zeros. It's the most direct approach — understanding the Riemann zeta function through the lens of complex analysis and analytic number theory.

Base Camp 1: Complex Analysis & Foundational Tools

Master the analytic foundations: complex function theory (contour integrals, analytic continuation) and arithmetic functions. A strong complex analysis background is essential.

  • Complex Analysis by Lars Ahlfors — Classic textbook covering contour integration, analytic continuation, and the tools needed to extend ζ(s) beyond Re(s) > 1.
  • Introduction to Analytic Number Theory by Tom Apostol — Highly regarded for basics of multiplicative functions and Dirichlet series. Rigorous yet clear.
  • Course in Arithmetic by Jean-Pierre Serre — Chapter VII on Dirichlet's theorem introduces characters and L-series (good preparation for generalized RH).

Base Camp 2: Prime Number Theorem & Basic Zeta Theory

Learn classical analytic results: the Prime Number Theorem (PNT) and basic properties of the Riemann zeta function — Euler products, functional equation, trivial zeros.

  • Multiplicative Number Theory by Harold Davenport — A classic covering PNT and Dirichlet L-series with legendary exposition.
  • An Introduction to the Theory of the Riemann Zeta-Function by S.J. Patterson — An accessible route into zeta-function theory covering PNT and the functional equation.
  • Introduction to Analytic Number Theory by Chandrasekharan — Takes a "scenic route" to PNT with rich historical insights.

Base Camp 3: The Riemann Zeta Function — Zeros & Key Theorems

Deep-dive into the zeta function's properties and zeros. Study analytic continuation of ζ(s), the functional equation, zero-free regions, and classical results like Hadamard's theorem.

  • Riemann's Zeta Function by H.M. Edwards — A Dover classic devoted to Riemann's 1859 paper and developments inspired by it. Often the first specialized book people read on RH.
  • The Theory of the Riemann Zeta-Function by Titchmarsh (2nd ed. revised by Heath-Brown) — The definitive advanced monograph covering zero-free regions, zero-density estimates, and the argument principle.
  • The Riemann Hypothesis: A Resource for the Afficionado and Virtuoso Alike edited by Peter Borwein et al. (2008) — Reprints important papers (Hardy's, Montgomery's) and includes expository articles.

Base Camp 4: Advanced Analytic Techniques & Partial Progress

Learn powerful tools used to attack RH: the explicit formula relating primes to zeros, zero-density theorems, and results like 40% of zeros on the critical line.

  • Multiplicative Number Theory: I. Classical Theory by Hugh Montgomery & Robert Vaughan — A modern authoritative text introducing contour integration, Vinogradov's method, and zero-density results.
  • Analytic Number Theory by Henryk Iwaniec & Emmanuel Kowalski — Graduate-level text covering advanced sieve methods, exponential sums, and L-functions.
  • The Riemann Zeta-Function: Theory and Applications by Aleksandar Ivić — Deep results on moments and density theorems, showing "almost all nontrivial zeros lie within an arbitrarily small distance of the 1/2 line."

Base Camp 5: Heuristics & Emerging Techniques

Understand new viewpoints treating primes (or Möbius function) as "random" to justify RH heuristically.

  • Prime Numbers and the Riemann Hypothesis by Barry Mazur & William Stein — A short, insight-rich book introducing RH gently with deep explanations.
  • Multiplicative Number Theory: The Pretentious Approach by Andrew Granville & K. Soundararajan — Cultivates the intuition that the Möbius function and primes behave randomly on average.
  • Prime Obsession by John Derbyshire — A remarkably clear step-by-step journey to RH for non-experts, including historical context.
