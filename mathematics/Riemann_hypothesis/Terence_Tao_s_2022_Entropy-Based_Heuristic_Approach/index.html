🎲 Path 8: Terence Tao's 2022 Entropy-Based Heuristic Approach

A novel perspective leveraging entropy, randomness, and partial randomness in the primes.

In 2022, Terence Tao sketched a fresh heuristic approach to RH, using ideas of entropy and randomness in prime distribution. While not a full proof, this approach has attracted significant interest as a new way to think about the problem — viewing the primes through a probabilistic lens and asking whether any deviation from RH would introduce too much irregularity.

Base Camp 1: Tao's "Just Barely True" Perspective & De Bruijn–Newman Constant

In 2018, Tao (with Brad Rodgers) proved the de Bruijn–Newman constant Λ ≥ 0. This implies that if RH is true at all, it is "barely true" — the zeros just line up on Re(s)=1/2 with no room to spare.

  • The De Bruijn–Newman constant is non-negative by Rodgers & Tao (2018, Duke Math. J.) — Proves that a certain heat flow evolves the zeros continuously; if you perturb the zeta kernel slightly, zeros off the line appear immediately.
  • Tao's Blog Post (Jan 2018) — Explains Newman's insight: "the Riemann Hypothesis, if true, is only just barely true."
  • Heuristic argument for the Riemann Hypothesis (MathOverflow) — Discussion capturing the theme that primes being random in certain ways yields RH.

Base Camp 2: Entropy in Number Theory

Grasp the entropy concepts Tao applies. He assigns an entropy to sequences (like the Möbius function) and studies how it changes, using techniques originally from additive combinatorics.

  • Tao's Blog: The Chowla conjecture is true on average (2015) — Introduces an entropy decrement argument for the Möbius function, defining entropy H(ℱ_N) for sequences and showing how to gradually reduce it.
  • Ergodic Theory and the Möbius Function by Peter Sarnak — Complements Tao's approach: the Möbius function has no long-range correlation, a fact implied by RH.

Base Camp 3: Tao's 2022 Outline & Related Work

Examine how the entropy heuristic connects to recent breakthroughs and primes at small scales.

  • Guth and Maynard's Breakthrough (2022, Annals of Math) — Proved that in any interval [X, X + X^0.525] there are roughly the expected number of primes, feeding into the entropy heuristic by showing primes behave "randomly enough" at small scales.
  • Randomness in the Prime Numbers by Terence Tao (UCLA lecture notes, 2021) — Describes how primes appear random in many statistical senses, setting the stage for the entropy approach.
