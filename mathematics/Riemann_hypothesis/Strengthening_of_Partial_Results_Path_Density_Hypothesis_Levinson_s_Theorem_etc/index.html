📊 Path 6: Strengthening of Partial Results

Pushing partial results as far as possible — proving more zeros on the line, constraining those off it ever tighter.

This path doesn't attack RH head-on, but aims to strengthen partial results until they meet RH itself. Key landmarks: Hardy's theorem (infinitely many zeros on the line, 1914), Levinson's 1/3 result (1974), Conrey's 2/5 (1989), and now 41.7% by Pratt et al. (2020).

Base Camp 1: Zero-Free Regions & Conditional Results

Understand classical results about where zeros cannot lie. The classical zero-free region: no zeros for Re(s) > 1 − c/log|t|.

  • The Distribution of Zeros of the Zeta Function in Titchmarsh — Chapter IX proves the classical zero-free region due to de la Vallée-Poussin.
  • Topics in Analytic Number Theory by Helmut Maier — Modern discussion of zero-free regions, density theorems, and connections to primes in short intervals.
  • Classical and Large Sieve Inequalities in Montgomery & Vaughan — Shows how sieve methods bound the density of zeros off the line, introducing the Density Hypothesis.

Base Camp 2: Hardy–Littlewood & Selberg's Results

Early breakthroughs on zeros on the line. In 1914, G.H. Hardy proved infinitely many zeros lie on Re(s)=1/2. Later, Selberg showed a positive proportion are on the line.

  • Hardy's Original Paper (1914) — Used Euler's product and Jensen's formula to show ζ(1/2+it) has infinitely many sign changes, implying zeros on the line.
  • On the Zeros of the Zeta Function by Atle Selberg (1942) — Developed mollifiers (short resonant Dirichlet polynomials) to show a positive proportion of zeros are on or near the line.
  • Harmony of the Zeta Zeros by Conrey & Iwaniec (Notices of the AMS, 2007) — Celebrates advances in proving proportions of zeros on the line.

Base Camp 3: Levinson's Theorem & Successors

Levinson (1974) shocked the community by proving at least one-third of zeros lie on the critical line. Conrey improved this to two-fifths (40%), and recent work pushes beyond 41%.

  • Levinson's Original Paper (Adv. Math. 1974) — Uses zeros of ζ'(s) to trap zeros of ζ(s) via mollifiers and the argument principle.
  • Montgomery's Lectures on the Riemann Zeta Function (Cambridge, 1986) — Explains Levinson's method step by step.
  • More than 41% of Zeros on the Line by Pratt, Robles, Zaharescu & Zeindler (2020) — The current frontier: five-twelfths of zeros on the line.

Base Camp 4: Zero Density Hypotheses & Consequences

The complementary approach: assume RH is false but zeros are scarce off-line, and prove conditional results. Many theorems assume a Density Hypothesis (far weaker than RH) and achieve nearly-RH level results.

  • The Number of Zeros of ζ(s) in Regions by Ingham (1932) — Proved early zero-density estimates showing "almost all nontrivial zeros are arbitrarily close to 1/2."
  • Density Theorems for Zeta and L-functions by Henryk Iwaniec — Modern account using large sieve and spectral methods for zero-density results.
  • The Generalized Riemann Hypothesis by Peter Sarnak (Contemporary Mathematics, 2008) — Surveys partial progress and the community consensus that evidence for RH is strong.
