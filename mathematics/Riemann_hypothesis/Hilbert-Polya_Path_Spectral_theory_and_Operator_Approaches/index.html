🎻 Path 2: Hilbert–Pólya — Spectral Theory & Operator Approaches

The conjecture that nontrivial zeros of ζ(s) correspond to eigenvalues of a self-adjoint operator.

The Hilbert–Pólya conjecture proposes a spectral interpretation of the zeros, mixing number theory with linear operators and quantum mechanics. If true, it would explain why the zeros lie on the critical line — eigenvalues of Hermitian operators are always real.

Base Camp 1: Spectral Theory Fundamentals

Acquire the language of operators and spectra: self-adjoint operators, eigenvalues, and Hilbert space.

  • Functional Analysis by Walter Rudin — Covers Hilbert spaces, self-adjoint compact operators, and spectral theory.
  • Spectral Theory of Linear Operators by Konrad Schmüdgen — Targeted introduction to unbounded self-adjoint operators.
  • Fourier Analysis and Self-Adjointness by Michael Reed & Barry Simon (Vol. II) — Standard reference detailing how quantum Hamiltonians have real spectra.

Base Camp 2: Analogies in Proven Cases (Function Fields & Selberg Zeta)

Study contexts where RH analogues are true and see how eigenvalues appear as "proof of concept" for Hilbert–Pólya.

  • Number Theory in Function Fields by Michael Rosen — Introduces zeta functions of function fields and Weil's results, showing how Frobenius eigenvalues sit on a circle.
  • The Selberg Trace Formula by Dennis Hejhal — Shows how nontrivial zeros of the Selberg zeta are eigenvalues of the Laplacian.
  • Automorphic Forms and Eigenvalues of the Laplacian by Henryk Iwaniec — Context on Selberg's theorem and how it parallels classical RH.

Base Camp 3: Proposed Operators & Quantum Analogies

Examine attempts to find an operator for the Riemann zeta itself — the heart of Hilbert–Pólya.

  • Berry–Keating Conjecture — The Hamiltonian H = XP (position × momentum) whose quantization might yield zeta zeros as eigenvalues. Read "Primes, Quantum Chaos and the Riemann Zeta Function" by Barry Cipra.
  • Noncommutative Geometry by Alain Connes — Proposes a spectral realization of zeros using adelic and noncommutative geometry.
  • Spectral Interpretation of the Riemann Zeta Function by B. Bagchi and P. Mazumdar (2017) — Surveys various operator proposals and obstacles.

Base Camp 4: Random Matrix Theory & Montgomery–Odlyzko Evidence

Montgomery (1973) showed the statistical distribution of zeros matches eigenvalues of large random Hermitian matrices — lending credence to the existence of a Hermitian operator.

  • Random Matrices, Frobenius Eigenvalues, and Monodromy by Nicholas Katz & Peter Sarnak — Draws deep parallels between random matrix ensembles and zero distributions.
  • The Music of the Primes by Marcus du Sautoy — Explains the Montgomery–Dyson meeting and the spectral hint in accessible terms.
  • Montgomery's Pair Correlation of Zeta Zeros in The Millennium Prize Problems (Clay Institute, 2006) — Article by Brian Conrey summarizing why mathematicians believe zeros might be eigenvalues.
