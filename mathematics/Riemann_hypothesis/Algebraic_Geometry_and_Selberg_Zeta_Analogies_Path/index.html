🔷 Path 4: Algebraic Geometry & Selberg Zeta Analogies

Drawing analogies from the Weil conjectures, Deligne's proof, and Selberg's spectral zeta to inspire approaches to classical RH.

This path mimics the proofs of RH analogues in algebraic geometry and automorphic forms. The Weil conjectures (proved by Deligne) give an RH analogue for varieties over finite fields. Selberg proved an RH analogue for his zeta function. Both successes inspire the hope of importing cohomological and trace formula methods into the number field case.

Base Camp 1: The Weil Conjectures & Function Field RH

Learn how RH is proved in algebraic geometry. For curves over finite fields, Weil proved the analogue of RH by showing eigenvalues of Frobenius have the right absolute value.

  • Dix Exposés sur la Cohomologie des Schémas (SGA 5) — The chapter by Serre on Zeta and L-functions introduces the zeta function of a variety and sketches the cohomological proof strategy.
  • Algebraic Number Theory by Jürgen Neukirch — Chapter VII discusses zeta functions of number fields vs. function fields, building intuition that Spec(ℤ) might play the role of a "curve over 𝔽₁."
  • Riemann Hypothesis in Characteristic p by K. Rubin and A. Silverberg — A digestible account of why RH holds for function field zeta functions.

Base Camp 2: Selberg Trace Formula & Zeta for Modular Forms

Selberg proved that for certain modular forms, all nontrivial eigenvalues of the Laplacian lie in [1/4, ∞), which translates to the Selberg zeta having zeros on Re(s)=1/2 — essentially an RH analogue.

  • Automorphic Forms and the Selberg Trace Formula by A. Knapp — Introduces Selberg's spectral theory on GL(2) and derives the Selberg zeta function, showing the parallel between the trace formula and the explicit formula.
  • Eigenvalues of the Laplacian and Modular Forms by Henryk Iwaniec — Provides intuition on Laplacian eigenvalues and connects to the Selberg eigenvalue conjecture.
  • Explicit Formulae by Anton Deitmar — Treats side by side the explicit formula for number fields and the Selberg trace formula for surfaces.

Base Camp 3: Deninger's Cohomological Approach

Investigate bold attempts to create a "cohomology of Spec(ℤ)" — the dream of many. Deninger proposed a hypothetical cohomology theory for arithmetic schemes that would produce the right spectral behavior for zeta.

  • Some Analogies between Number Theory and Dynamical Systems by Christopher Deninger (1998) — Outlines how one might interpret log ζ(s) as a determinant of an infinite-dimensional operator, introducing the idea of a "mystery cohomology."
  • Noncommutative Geometry, Dynamical Systems and ζ by Alain Connes — Builds on Deninger's ideas using tools from noncommutative geometry to derive the explicit formula and interpret it as a trace formula.
  • Equivalents of the Riemann Hypothesis, Vol. 2: Analytic Equivalences by K. Broughan (2017) — Comprehensive catalog of RH-equivalent statements, including Weil's positivity criterion and geometric interpretations.
