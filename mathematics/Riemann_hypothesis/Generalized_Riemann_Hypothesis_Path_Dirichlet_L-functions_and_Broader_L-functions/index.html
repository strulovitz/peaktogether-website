🌐 Path 7: Generalized Riemann Hypothesis — Dirichlet L-functions & Broader L-functions

Broadening RH's scope to all L-functions of number fields, starting with Dirichlet L-series.

GRH asserts that all L-functions of number fields have zeros only on the critical line. Studying GRH often sheds light on RH itself, since many techniques and consequences are analogous. Sometimes working in the generalized setting gives additional insights by comparing different L-functions.

Base Camp 1: Dirichlet Characters & L-functions

Start with Dirichlet L-series, the simplest extension of ζ(s). GRH for Dirichlet L(s,χ) means nontrivial zeros on Re(s)=1/2 for every non-principal character χ.

  • Introduction to Analytic Number Theory by Tom Apostol — Chapters 7–9 introduce Dirichlet characters, L-functions, and prove Dirichlet's theorem on primes in arithmetic progressions.
  • Multiplicative Number Theory by Davenport — Advanced treatment of Dirichlet L-functions, proving zero-free regions and discussing GRH implications.
  • A Course in Arithmetic by J-P. Serre — Chapter 3 connects Dirichlet L-functions to characters of Galois groups via class field theory.

Base Camp 2: Equivalences & Consequences of GRH

Learn what GRH would imply: tight bounds for prime gaps in progressions, class number estimates, effective Chebotarev density theorem, and more.

  • Problems in Analytic Number Theory by M. Ram Murty — Problem-driven approach proving that GRH implies sharpened Pólya–Vinogradov bounds and best-possible prime number estimates for arithmetic progressions.
  • The Grand Riemann Hypothesis by G. Cornell (1997) — Survey describing GRH for various L-functions and collecting consequences.
  • Equivalents of the Riemann Hypothesis, Vol. 2 by K. Broughan — Includes analytic equivalents for Dirichlet L-functions, showing patterns that unify RH and GRH criteria.

Base Camp 3: Advanced L-functions & Deep Analogies

Dive into the general theory of L-functions (GL(2) and beyond) and the Langlands program. GRH extends to all automorphic L-functions.

  • Automorphic Forms and L-functions for the General Linear Group by D. Bump — Introduces L-functions of higher degree and states the generalized RH for them.
  • L-functions and Random Matrix Theory by J. Keating & N. Snaith — Links GRH to random matrix theory, postulating universality underlying all GRH cases.

Base Camp 4: Cutting-Edge Partial Results towards GRH

Modern number theory has achieved subconvexity bounds and averaged results that approach GRH-level conclusions.

  • Subconvexity for L-functions (Lectures by Iwaniec and Sarnak) — Subconvexity bounds indirectly tell us about zero distributions and spacing.
  • Gaps between Primes: The Impacts of GRH by Andrew Granville (2015) — Explains how much easier prime gap problems would be with GRH, measuring progress toward GRH through unconditional tools.
