# Chapter 7 — Emotional Shading (incl. Psychoacoustic Triggers of Fear, Tension, and Excitement)

> Audience-corrected version: the game is for ~20-year-old players (NOT a kids' game), so the full emotional/tension palette from Part C is in scope.

## Revised Ch.7 game notes (audience: ~20-year-olds) 🎮

- Part C is now fully usable, not something to soften. The fear/tension toolbox is exactly what makes games gripping for adult players:
  - Roughness band (20–150 Hz AM) — fair game for failed casts, corrupted spells, or menacing entities. Worth the DSP effort after all.
  - Tritones, minor 2nds, clusters — real dissonance for real stakes, not gentle wobbles.
  - Full BPM range: exploration ~80–110, combat/challenge 120–160, and yes — >160 + dissonance chaotic-panic tier for climactic boss spells (the DOOM/Dead Space playbook).
- Dark spell tier becomes a core feature, not an easter egg: a "forbidden spellbook" of functions rendered with the horror palette — the Dalek-style ring-mod, Jaws-style accelerating semitones — is a legitimate design pillar now. 🖤
- The Resident Evil save-room contrast still applies (it's an adult-game pattern anyway): calm study area ↔ tense casting encounters. Contrast is what makes the tension land.
- What stays unchanged: consonance/dissonance as instant feedback, evaluative conditioning (BRECVEMA) as the learning loop, mode-as-data (minor when f<0), adaptive tempo — these were audience-independent.

## Chapter content recap (the book itself)

- Simple approach: valence → major vs. minor scale; arousal → tempo/dynamics. Theory anchors: Hevner's Adjective Circle (1930s), Russell's Circumplex (valence × arousal, 1980).
- Part C deep-dive — psychoacoustic triggers of fear/tension:
  - Roughness & nonlinear noise (20–150 Hz AM) mimic animal distress calls → amygdala fear response (Blumstein 2010, Arnal 2015).
  - Pitch glides / looming sounds (rising sirens, Psycho glissandi); amplitude modulation (4–7 Hz trembling → tens of Hz roughness); heartbeat imitation.
  - Tempo entrainment with heart rate (~60–100 BPM resting; faster = higher arousal; Gomez & Danuser 2007).
  - Beyond major/minor: cultural conditioning (Smit 2022); dissonant intervals — minor 2nd, tritone ("Devil's Interval"), tone clusters, minor 9th.
  - Case studies: Jaws (E–F semitone), Psycho strings, Daleks (ring-mod ~30 Hz); games: Silent Hill, Resident Evil, Dead Space (A.L.I.V.E.), DOOM 2016.
  - Frameworks: Juslin's BRECVEMA (8 mechanisms) + Zentner's GEMS (9 emotion clusters incl. Tension).
