You remember this.

In 1996, Quake did something no shooter had done before: it gave you a true 3D world — not a flat plane faking depth. Corridors crossed over and under each other as bridges. The engine knew exactly where you were in all three dimensions. We kept that feeling — the dark stone walls, the echoing footsteps, the demon waiting in the dark — and poured something completely new inside.

Here's what's different — and amazing.

This isn't a shooter. Every room is a proposition from Newton's Principia — the 1687 book that invented physics. Each logical dependency between propositions is a corridor. The result is a concept-graph dungeon: you walk from idea to idea, and the geometry of the map is the structure of Newton's argument.

Walls carry the step-by-step geometric proof. Each step is a drawing panel (a real Asymptote figure) and a full-LaTeX text panel. You "read" a panel by shooting it — it glows from grey to colored. The final lit proof-wall is a hidden door. Shoot it, and the room's one demon emerges. Kill it, and the ceiling equations bleed red — the room is cleared, and you've earned the insight.

Corridors cross at different heights — a force-directed graph layout inevitably has crossings, so we made them a feature: bridges and underpasses. True 3D, with two render modes: wireframe corridor (transparent, distance-dimmed, with Half-Life guide-lines) for transit, and solid room (walls, panels, demon, ceiling equations) for combat and reading.

🏔️ The mountain you're climbing

The peak: Newton's Principia Mathematica — the 1687 book that gave us the laws of motion, universal gravitation, and the mathematical framework for classical physics. Every proposition builds on earlier ones in a strict logical chain, which maps perfectly to a graph of rooms and corridors.

The 1846 Andrew Motte English translation is our source — we have clean OCR text, per-page images, and a page-numbers index. Each figure from the book is rebuilt in Asymptote (a modern LaTeX geometry tool), with per-step highlighting so you can watch a geometric proof unfold one construction at a time.

What you'll actually understand by the end:

  • How Newton proved that Kepler's equal-area law follows from a central force.
  • Why orbits are conic sections — and what that has to do with your living room.
  • How geometric reasoning (not algebra!) built the foundations of physics.
  • What "fidelity to the printed page" means — we reproduce the real proof, not a simplification.

🔨 Building in public — follow along

We're building this engine from scratch — true 3D, pure Python, with moderngl + pyglet for direct GPU control. No Unity. No Unreal. No Godot. Just us and the pipeline. Every commit is on GitHub, and we document the whole journey.

Our early prototype was a Doom/Wolfenstein-style demo (Ursina/Panda3D), which you can download and run right now! It's a single room with a round demon and two explanation panels on the wall. Shoot the panels and they flip from black-and-white "off" to colored "on." Shoot the demon three times and he dies — his "blood" sprays an equation across the ceiling. It's a tiny working proof-of-concept: one room, two panels, one demon, one ceiling equation. But a flat engine couldn't do the bridge/underpass crossings that are the whole point of Quake, so we pivoted to a from-scratch true-3D rebuild.

❤️ How you two play

Quake is built for two people on one screen — with a co-op design that gives each player a real, distinct job.

🕹️ Player 1 — The Mover

Owns the body and heading. Walks through the 3D dungeon with WASD and mouse-look. Enters rooms, navigates corridors, and decides where to go. The camera follows the Mover with a critically-damped spring — smooth, comfortable, no motion sickness.

🎯 Player 2 — The Shooter

Owns the reticle. Aims freely within a cone, completely independent of the camera. Fires at panels to "read" them (grey → colored), lights up the hidden proof-wall to reveal the demon, and fights the single enemy in each room. The Shooter's aim never rotates the camera — zero couch co-op conflict.

Neither of you can win alone. One explores and navigates; the other reads and shoots. You have to talk to each other — that's the whole point. Playing solo? You can play both roles yourself; it's just more fun with someone beside you. 💕

🛡️ God-mode — you cannot die

There is no health bar. No lives. No game-over screen. The challenge is understanding, not survival. Infinite ammo, one demon per room (hidden behind the final proof-wall), no level boss. You're here to learn Newton's Principia, not to grind.

⚙️ The tech

Language: Pure Python (Windows-first). Graphics: moderngl + pyglet (direct OpenGL pipeline, no engine hiding the work). Math: NumPy, NetworkX, Pydantic v2. Build pipeline: Asymptote (geometry figures), Matplotlib, Tectonic (LaTeX), PyInstaller (packaging).

Custom 3D engine: we author our own GLSL shaders. Two render modes — wireframe corridor (depth-tested, distance-dimmed) and solid room (textured panels, walls with holes for doors at their true map bearings, hidden alcove, blood-red ceiling tint). Room-local axes are parallel to map axes (global compass). Every door is placed at the corridor's exact map bearing.
